Everything you need to know about charging an EV

New to electric vehicles? This guide covers the types of chargers, how fast they are, how to charge step by step, and tips to make every trip easier.

Types of chargers

Slow

Home / Wall socket

Plugs into a regular socket. Great for overnight charging of scooters, bikes and small cars. Slowest option, but the cheapest and most convenient.

AC · Medium

AC charger (Type 2)

Found at homes, offices, malls and hotels. Good for charging while you work, shop or stay somewhere for a few hours.

DC · Fast

DC fast charger (CCS2)

Found on highways and city hubs. Best for long trips when you need to top up quickly. Not every vehicle supports it, so check yours first.

How long does it take?

Rough guide for a car. Real times depend on your battery size and vehicle.

Charger Typical power Roughly how long Best for
Wall socket About 3 kW Many hours (overnight) Home, bikes and scooters
AC (Type 2) About 7 to 22 kW A few hours Work, malls, hotels
DC fast (CCS2) 30 kW and above Around 30 to 60 min for 20% to 80% Highway trips

How to charge, step by step

1

Find a compatible charger

Search by city or PIN code and filter by your charger type.

2

Check the status

Make sure the station is open and the charger is free before you go.

3

Park and plug in

Park close to the charger, switch the car off, and connect the plug to the charging port.

4

Start the session

Use the station's app, a QR code or an RFID card, then wait for charging to begin.

5

Stop and unplug

End the session in the app or on the screen, then remove the plug and put it back neatly.

Handy tips

Charge 20% to 80%

Fast charging slows down near full, so stopping at 80% saves time on trips.

Plan your route

Check chargers along the way before you leave, and keep a backup option.

Carry your cable

Some AC stations need your own cable. Keep it in the boot.

Be kind at stations

Move your car once you're done so the next person can charge.
